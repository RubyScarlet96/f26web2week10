Flamingos are wading birds known for their long legs, curved bills, and vivid pink feathers. Their coloring comes from pigments in the algae and small crustaceans they eat.

They often gather in large flocks in shallow lakes and lagoons, where they filter-feed with their bills. Flamingos may stand on one leg while resting, helping them conserve body heat.

Chameleons are remarkable reptiles that spend much of their lives moving quietly through trees and shrubs, gripping narrow branches with feet that work like tiny hands and balancing with long, curling tails. Their independently moving eyes can scan different parts of their surroundings, helping them notice insects, other chameleons, and possible danger without turning their heads. When a chameleon spots a meal, it may slowly approach before shooting out its long, sticky tongue with surprising speed to catch an insect. Many people think chameleons change color only to blend into their background, but their colors can also reflect temperature, mood, health, and communication with other chameleons. A relaxed individual might appear green or brown among leaves, while a more alert or agitated one may show brighter patterns. These colors are produced by layers in the skin that interact with light, rather than by simply painting the body to match every surface. Chameleons are found in a variety of habitats, including rainforests, dry woodlands, and scrubby landscapes, and different species have adapted to the conditions around them. Some are small enough to rest on a fingertip, while others grow much larger and display impressive crests, horns, or vivid bands. They usually rely on camouflage and careful movement instead of chasing prey over long distances, and their patient behavior makes them fascinating to observe. Like all wild animals, chameleons need suitable shelter, warmth, humidity, and food, so they should be appreciated in their natural habitats or cared for only by people who understand their specialized needs. Their unusual eyes, precise tongues, grasping feet, and shifting colors combine to make chameleons some of the most distinctive reptiles in the animal world.
